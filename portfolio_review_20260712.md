# Portfolio review, aebauer.dev

Reviewed 2026-07-12 for Alex Bauer, ahead of week-one applications to Anthropic FDE Applied AI, Glean Founding FDE, ElevenLabs Enterprise SE, Ibotta Principal ML, and Guild Sr SWE II.

## 1. Executive summary

The site's bones are good. The visual system, typography, dark aesthetic, and the "Builder and Client-Side Operator" story all work, and Seraphone is a genuinely credible headline project with two live demo phone lines and a real supporting product site at seraphone.ai. However, the portfolio is not shippable in its current state. Three of four projects show "SCREENSHOT COMING SOON," the Option Overlay project link (isadora.ai) returns an Application Error, the R Machine Learning card describes a heart-disease classifier and RAG boilerplate but actually links to a completely different Atlas of Machine Understanding site, and the project ordering does not match the intended narrative. The biggest risk is that a reviewer clicks the second or third project, hits a broken page or a placeholder, and concludes the "shipped" claim is aspirational. Fixing the four items below turns this into a strong portfolio in about a day of work.

## 2. Must-fix before applying

1. **Cut Option Overlay from the portfolio or hide its live link.** isadora.ai returns "Application Error" as the page title with a blank white viewport. If a reviewer clicks "View project site," they see a broken product. Either remove the card, mark it clearly as private/in-progress with the "View project site" button removed, or point it to a private-repo README with a screenshot instead of a live URL. Estimate: 20 minutes.

2. **Reorder the project grid to Seraphone, then Atlas/ML, then RAPTOR, then Option Overlay (if kept).** The DOM order is currently Seraphone, RAPTOR, Option Overlay, R Machine Learning. Your stated priority is Seraphone, ML with UMAP and Gaussian splat, RAPTOR, then Option Overlay as WIP. The reader sees the top-left card first, then top-right, then reads left-to-right down. Whatever is second matters, and right now that slot is going to RAPTOR (with a placeholder screenshot) instead of the Atlas (your visually strongest asset). Estimate: 15 minutes.

3. **Rewrite the R Machine Learning card to match what actually ships at ml.aebauer.dev.** The card promises "a heart-disease classifier and RAG over the legacy datasets, powered by Vertex AI (Gemini)." What visitors actually find is a beautifully designed "Atlas of Machine Understanding, Vol. 01" with a UMAP projection of 12,000 papers, RAPTOR-derived regions, kernel-density relief, filters by source and year, a 3D view, and a full /map page with labeled regions like "LLM Mechanics," "Metaphysics, Mind, and Epistemic Foundations," and "Health Research, Disease Mechanisms, and Prediction." The current card undersells the actual work by roughly an order of magnitude, and the mismatch between promise and payoff will read as either sloppy or misleading. Rename to something like "An Atlas of Machine Understanding" or "ML portfolio, Vol. 01," rewrite the description to lead with the UMAP embedding atlas of 12k papers, RAPTOR clustering, and the agent that decomposes and cites, and keep the "legacy R work preserved as Foundations" as a secondary detail. Estimate: 45 minutes.

4. **Replace all three "SCREENSHOT COMING SOON" placeholders with real screenshots.** Only Seraphone has a screenshot in its expand panel. RAPTOR, Option Overlay, and R Machine Learning all show the same "SCREENSHOT COMING SOON" empty-state graphic. Placeholders that repeat three times signal "unfinished." For RAPTOR, a screenshot of the codebase analyzer with a project loaded (chat panel plus knowledge tree plus Project Map) is the shot to use. For the Atlas, the /map view with the labeled regions and density contours is the money shot. For Option Overlay, either add a screenshot or cut the project. Estimate: 45 minutes total.

2. A "last shipped" or "last updated" date near the footer or on each project card, so a reviewer can see the work is recent (October and November 2026 activity).

## 5. Project-by-project notes

### Seraphone

This is the strongest asset on the site and reads well. The card copy is specific ("Multi-tenant AI answering service replacing IVR for healthcare and professional services. Real-time voice, HIPAA-compliant, PostgreSQL knowledge graph, Twilio-backed"), the two demo phone numbers are gold and belong exactly where they are, the stack tags are appropriate, the expand panel has a real screenshot showing the seraphone.ai control panel with knowledge base and knowledge graph views, and the "About this project" copy explains the HIPAA posture at three specific layers (application-level encryption, PII scrubbing, end-to-end encrypted SMS callbacks). The "View project site" button leads to a polished product page at seraphone.ai with a hero "Stop missing important calls," a phone mockup showing a live conversation, and clear "Get started" and "See it in action" CTAs. Nothing to fix here except potentially adding a "How it works" or architecture diagram inside the expand panel for the technical reader.

### R Machine Learning (should be renamed to Atlas of Machine Understanding)

This is the largest gap between promise and delivery on the site. The card promises a modest ML boilerplate with a heart-disease classifier and RAG chat. The actual site is much more ambitious: a full editorial "atlas" styled as Vol. 01 of a hand-set volume, with a frontispiece, a Map, a Guide, Foundations, and a Colophon. The Map view alone (12,000 papers, kernel-density relief, RAPTOR-derived regions, filters by source and date range, a color-by-region or discipline toggle, and a "View in 3D" affordance) is the single most impressive artifact on your portfolio. The problem is that unless the reviewer clicks "View project site" and then scrolls or refreshes, they never see it, because the card describes a different project entirely and the frontispiece plate is initially empty. Fix the card copy, feature the Map screenshot, and this jumps from fourth-billed to arguably the site's headline for ML-heavy readers (Anthropic, Ibotta).

### RAPTOR

The concept ("Tree-sitter parses a codebase into ASTs; RAPTOR builds a hierarchical semantic graph; a chat engine answers architecture-level questions") is compelling and the sample questions in the expand panel ("where does the auth boundary live?", "what gets touched if I rename this table?") are exactly the right anchors. The site at kg.aebauer.dev is a real product with Chat/Tree views, project selection, sources panel, and a Project Map. But the landing experience needs work. The first-load state shows "Loading conversation..." and an empty chat with no visible next step, which is not the demo you want a distracted recruiter to see. Once you pick a project from the dropdown, the "How it all fits together" panel appears and the demo is impressive, so getting a visitor to that state in one click is the highest-leverage fix. Add a screenshot to the card while you're at it, ideally a state showing the Project Map, the chat panel with a good answer, and the sources sidebar populated.

### Option Overlay Recommender

Currently the weakest project on the site, and the reason isn't the concept, which is fine (vector embeddings of holdings and strategies, historical outcomes, FastAPI service for advisors), it's execution. There is no screenshot, no live site (isadora.ai returns Application Error), the description is more abstract than the other three, and the "why" is stronger in your own head than on the page. Recommendation: cut this project from the portfolio for the week-one applications. The rationale is straightforward. Three shipped systems with working demos beats four projects where one is broken, and the reviewer who sees the broken link is going to remember it. If you want to keep the wealth-management thread visible, mention Option Overlay Recommender inside the About paragraph (you already do) but do not link out to a broken site. You can bring it back as a full card once isadora.ai is stable and you have a screenshot. Alternative if you want to keep it: rename the "View project site" button to "Coming soon" or "Available on request," add a real architecture diagram or terminal-recording GIF in place of the screenshot placeholder, and be explicit that this is in-progress.

## 6. Company-lens read

**Anthropic FDE Applied AI.** Reads well overall. Seraphone maps directly to enterprise AI-delivery work: real customers, HIPAA constraints, a knowledge graph, live phone lines. The "translate them for the people paying the bill" tagline is essentially the FDE job description, and the About paragraph (client-facing work with financial advisors, wealth managers, trust attorneys, medical device support) is exactly the operating profile Anthropic wants. The risks: the Atlas is undersold in its current framing, so an ML-curious reviewer might not click through and see the strongest asset, and the broken Option Overlay link is exactly the kind of detail an FDE evaluator will note as "does not close the loop." Fix the ordering and cut Option Overlay and this is a strong FDE portfolio.

**Glean Founding FDE.** RAPTOR is a bullseye match for Glean's mission (semantic search and retrieval over a codebase mirrors semantic search and retrieval over a company's docs and messages), and Seraphone provides the "I have shipped an enterprise-grade product with real users" credential a Founding FDE needs. The Atlas is also directly on-thesis for Glean, since UMAP-over-a-corpus is what a Founding FDE at Glean will spend a lot of time explaining to customers. The risk is the RAPTOR demo's cold-start experience: "Loading conversation..." with an empty pane and no obvious next action reads as unfinished. For a Founding FDE role where the candidate is expected to run customer demos, a demo that doesn't demo itself is a specific worry. Fix the landing state and this becomes the site's best asset for Glean.

**ElevenLabs Enterprise SE North America.** Seraphone plus the two live demo phone lines is essentially a pre-baked Enterprise SE interview loop. Voice, real-time, telephony, enterprise compliance posture, a working product page at seraphone.ai, and the fact that a hiring manager can literally pick up their phone and evaluate the product in ninety seconds. The Atlas and RAPTOR are less directly relevant for an SE role but signal the depth an Enterprise SE at ElevenLabs needs when they're translating between AI research and a healthcare CIO. The dual-career "client-side operator" framing is close to a perfect fit for this role. The Option Overlay broken link is a smaller issue here since the SE evaluator will focus on Seraphone, but still worth cleaning up.

**Ibotta Principal ML.** The read here is more mixed until you rewrite the Atlas card. As written, the R Machine Learning card describes a bootcamp-level RAG and classifier project, which is under the bar for a Principal ML role. Once rewritten to feature the 12k-paper embedding atlas, RAPTOR clustering, and the retrieval agent, it becomes a credible ML systems story. The About paragraph's "lifted forecast accuracy by 25% and feeding executive dashboards directly to decision-makers" is exactly the kind of business-outcome sentence Principal ML at a consumer app wants to see. Fix the card copy first, then this reads as a strong Principal candidate.

**Guild Sr SWE II.** Straight-ahead engineering read. The stack breadth (Python, FastAPI, PostgreSQL, pgvector, Vite, TypeScript, React, Twilio) is right for a senior full-stack role. Three shipped systems with a knowledge graph, a chat engine, and an embedding atlas is a strong engineering artifact set for Sr SWE II. The dual-persona framing (financial services and healthcare client-facing plus builder) fits Guild's mission (healthcare, education, workforce) especially well since Guild's customers are large employers whose HR and benefits teams are themselves "the people paying the bill." Adding one line per project about how it's tested or deployed (or a small "Ops" note in About) would help for the SWE II read specifically. The Option Overlay broken link is a bigger issue for Guild than the other companies, since a working link is table stakes for a senior engineer's own portfolio.

## 7. What to add before applying

1. **A short "Approach" or "How I work" section between Projects and About.** Two paragraphs, no lists. Something like: "I start with the customer conversation, not the model. Whether that customer is an advisor at a $2B RIA or a physician in an ICU, I'd rather ship a boring integration that reduces their call volume by 40% than a state-of-the-art model that requires a data scientist to babysit. The 'AI-powered systems' half of my work exists to make the 'translate them' half unnecessary." This is optional, but for FDE and Founding FDE roles specifically, it earns you five to ten seconds of goodwill before the reviewer opens Seraphone.

2. **A public GitHub link on the site.** The Contact section links to @alexander-e-bauer, which is fine, but consider a "Selected commits" or "Recent activity" area if the profile has visible repo activity.

3. **A single-file architecture diagram for Seraphone.** An SVG showing Twilio inbound to a FastAPI service, a knowledge graph query loop, an escalation path to a human, and an outbound encrypted SMS callback. Nothing fancy. Buys credibility for the "HIPAA-compliant from the data flow up" claim.

4. **Consider adding a "Case studies" one-liner per project.** Even a made-up-sounding-but-real "Deployed at Dock Resolutions Support" reference on the Seraphone card (the screenshot shows Dock Resolutions in the sidebar) grounds the work in a real customer rather than a demo tenant.

5. **A footer line like "Currently: [something specific about what you're building this week]"** shows the site is alive. Optional.

## Bottom line

You have a strong core here and the fixes are small in aggregate: cut or fix one broken link, reorder four cards, rewrite one card to match its actual site, add three screenshots, and repair two demo landing states. A day of focused work moves this portfolio from "will read as unfinished" to "closes the loop." Do not send applications until at least items 1, 2, 3, and 4 in the Must-fix list are done.
